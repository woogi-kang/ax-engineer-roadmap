# Leader and Governance Track

## Audience

For leaders and program owners who decide the AX portfolio, budget, organizational model, procurement, risk tolerance, and workflow accountability.

## Goal

Do not treat purchasing tools or announcing a company-wide initiative as outcomes. Build an organizational ability to deploy valuable workflows safely and expand, modify, or stop them based on operating evidence.

## 1. Portfolio priority

Do not rank candidates by expected benefit alone. Compare value, frequency, user, baseline, data readiness, risk, recoverability, owner, and adoption difficulty.

Decide:

- Start now versus hold
- Remove or simplify without AI
- Pilot budget and validation period
- Expand, maintain, modify, and stop criteria

Evidence:

- [Use-Case Scorecard](../toolkit/use-case-scorecard.md)
- Portfolio decision record
- Scope, non-goals, and stop conditions

## 2. Accountability and approval

Record actual decision rights rather than committee names:

```text
Workflow outcome and acceptance
Data-use approval
Deployment approval
External-action authority
Emergency stop authority
Retirement of old procedure
Vendor contract and exit
```

If two roles are accountable for one item, document conflict resolution. Use roles and backups rather than personal names.

Evidence:

- Responsibility matrix
- Approval and delegation rules
- Emergency stop and reporting path

## 3. Investment and operating criteria

Pilot cost is more than model usage. Include data cleanup, integration, evaluation, security review, UAT, training, support, operations, and vendor replacement.

Separate:

- Workflow outcome: completion, errors and rework, customer or employee impact, opportunity cost
- Operations: quality, latency, cost, incidents, approval wait, manual fallback
- Adoption: official-process use, correction burden, support, retirement of the old process

## 4. Procurement and vendor exit

Confirm before purchase:

- Ownership of organizational accounts and administrator access
- Input-data use, retention, and training terms
- Export format and deletion evidence
- Access to logs and audit records
- Model and vendor portability
- Workflow fallback and data handling after termination
- Legal review for subcontracting, cross-border transfer, and storage location

## 5. Adoption and role transition

Look for official workflow change rather than training sessions and seats:

- Do frontline users participate in design and acceptance?
- Are local support owners available?
- Are the new SOP and KPIs approved?
- Are parallel-operation and retirement criteria explicit?
- Can normal exceptions be handled without the central AX team?

## 6. When to build a shared foundation

Do not turn the first success into platform requirements. Promote only contracts and components actually reused in a second workflow.

Potential shared elements:

- Workflow and run identifiers
- Input and output schemas and versions
- Evaluation execution and records
- Approval, permission, and audit events
- Stop, recovery, and manual fallback
- Cost, quality, and adoption status

Elements that may stay team-specific:

- UI and user experience
- Domain terminology and rules
- Model and framework choice
- Source-system integration

## Evidence to produce

- AX portfolio and decision history
- Invest and stop criteria
- Accountability and approval matrix
- Procurement and vendor-exit checklist
- Pilot operating review
- Approved SOP, KPI, and role changes
- Reuse comparison from a second workflow

## Common failures

- Starting with an executive idea without validation
- Making the central team a permanent build, approval, and support bottleneck
- Buying a company-wide platform before finding workflows
- Treating legal and security as final approval steps
- Generalizing a good demo into enterprise impact
- Locking accounts and data into one vendor without an exit plan
